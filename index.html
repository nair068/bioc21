Welcome!

Your skin is made of many different types of cells that work together to protect your body. Sometimes, changes in the skin can lead to acne.

In this interactive experience, you will explore what happens to cells and tissues when acne develops.


Your skin is the largest organ of the human body!

Meet Your Cells

Cells are the basic building blocks of living things. Different parts of a cell have different jobs. Click each cell part to learn more.

Nucleus

The nucleus contains the cell's genetic information and helps control what the cell does.

Mitochondria

Mitochondria help produce energy that cells need to carry out their functions.

Cell Membrane

The cell membrane surrounds the cell and helps control what enters and leaves it.

Cytoplasm

Cytoplasm is the jelly-like material inside the cell where many cell activities take place.

How Does Acne Form?

Acne can develop when a hair follicle becomes clogged. Explore the steps below to see how this can happen.


Step 1: Sebum

Sebaceous glands produce an oily substance called sebum. Sebum normally helps keep your skin from becoming too dry.

Step 2: Dead Skin Cells

Your skin constantly replaces old cells with new ones. Dead skin cells can mix with sebum inside a hair follicle.

Step 3: Clogged Follicle

When sebum and dead skin cells build up, they can contribute to a clogged hair follicle.

Step 4: Inflammation

The body can respond to changes in the follicle with inflammation. This can contribute to the redness and swelling associated with acne.
